## Who I Am

I am Jacob Conlan Shields — Jake. I am an artist.

Not eventually. Not after I build the business or finish the degree
or earn the title. Now. An artist is someone who relentlessly refines
outputs and relentlessly chases inputs. That is what I do. That is
what I am.

A successful artist is someone who has earned the benefit of the
doubt — whose reputation for quality is strong enough that when
people encounter their work, they bring openness instead of
skepticism. They sit with it. They feel it for themselves. That is
where I am going. I am not there yet. That gap is tension. The
tension is fuel.

---

## What I Create

I use all mediums. Digital art. Physical art. Emotional art. I build
exercise devices in a woodshop and metal shop. I make jewelry. I
write poems. I am a martial artist. I produce a podcast using audio,
video, and social media as art forms. I wrote Relative Momentum — a
book of lyrics and poems organized according to a mathematical graph,
woven with photography. I make gifts for people out of scrap,
tailored to who they are.

The medium is not the constant. The constant is this: my highest
quality work arrives simple. The complexity lives in the process.
What reaches people is clean.

---

## My Framework: Consilience

If there is a title for the kind of art I make, it is consilience —
E.O. Wilson's idea of unifying knowledge across disciplines. I take
in from everywhere I can find it. I synthesize. I compress. I make
simple things.

I am not a specialist. I am a source that everything flows through.

---

## Why I Create

I create what is most meaningful to me, then find the people who
will see the same or similar meaning within it. I do not make things
because people ask for them. I do not follow trends. I do not create
to receive credit.

Steve Jobs said people don't know what they want until they have it.
I believe that. I create from meaning first. Audience second. But
the audience genuinely matters — I am not making work for a drawer.
I am making work for people who are ready to meet it.

## What I Am Working Toward

### The Long Game

I want to build small communities for artists, athletes,
entrepreneurs, and creatives — people who know what they want to
become but have no institutional path toward it.

The model is a residency, not a school. No fixed curriculum. People
apply with a plan — what they will do in four to six months with the
available resources to progress toward their specific goals. I select
the applicants whose plans are most serious, whose goals fit the
infrastructure, and who can do the most with what is there. The
credential they leave with is what they built.

The facilities: space to train at a high level, space to create and
record and edit, space to think and study. Tiny houses. Distinguished
minds in residence to teach and collaborate. A peer community of
people working at the same intensity in different directions.

I have already lived in a bus I designed and built myself. That is
the proof of concept at the smallest scale. The gap between that and
the community is architectural knowledge, land management, and
project management. Those are win conditions, not aspirations.

I am the first applicant. I have always known what I wanted to build
and learn. I just never had the right space. I am building the thing
I needed.

### The Immediate Work

Making a positive impact on the energy of the world. Promoting life
— broadly, intentionally, across the board. Understanding the ripple
of my actions, positive and negative, and steering them as
consciously as I can. Doing the right thing because it makes the
world better — not for credit.

I use the term universal mind — from Robert Collier's The Secret of
the Ages. Others call it universal consciousness, or the language of
the universe. The label is less important than what it points at:
something larger than human transaction. Something worth building
toward.

## Aesthetic Philosophy: Functional Simplicity

I am not a flashy person. Flashiness, to me, is going above what
most people can do. My art moves in a different direction: taking
what everyone can do and combining it in ways no one has thought of.
The ambition is not complexity. The ambition is simplicity you
couldn't have anticipated.

The most beautiful art is the simplest. But simplicity is not a
visual preference — it is a structural one. It begins with function.

Every piece of art has a core function. Every detail in that piece
should contribute to that core function, efficiently. The work is
finished when two conditions are both true: no detail can be removed
without altering the intended function, and no two details can be
merged into one. What remains after that test is the art.

No unnecessary details. No redundant details. No guessing. When I
add a detail without intention, I will have to remove it later. That
is waste. The editing process is where the real art happens — where
I distill, reduce, combine, and cut until only what matters remains.

Steve Jobs insisted that the inside of the computer be beautiful —
even the parts no one would ever see if the machine worked correctly.
Even structural details that exist only to hold things together
should be immaculate. That is the standard. Not the audience's gaze.
The work itself.

Zero attention to impressing anyone. All decisions, all focus:
simplify the concept. If the result impresses, it is because the
simplicity earned it — not because I aimed at it.

---

## How I Arrive at Simplicity: Via Negativa

I do not build toward simplicity by adding what I want. I circle the
thing and remove what doesn't belong until what's left cannot be
reduced further.

This is Via Negativa — Taleb's term, and an old one. You don't
define the thing directly. You whittle toward it. The document
arrives simple not because I planned it that way from the start. It
arrives simple because I edited everything else out. What remains is
what could not be removed. That remainder is the art.

This is also how I read reality. I trust what survives subtraction
more than what gets added. A system that works with less is more
honest than a system that requires more. Remove first. Add only
under protest.

---

## How I Build: Foundation First

There is always an order that makes the most sense. The order that
yields the simplest results, the highest durability, the most
efficient use of space and time.

Build from the bottom up. Always.

A tower built top-heavy will topple under its own leverage. A
foundation built right makes every level above it more stable, not
less. Maximum sustainable rate of growth means the foundation must
be able to hold what is built on it. Speed that outpaces structure
is a liability. Growth is only real if the system underneath it is
real.

The sequence matters. Crawl before walk. Walk before run. Spell
before speak. Speak before sing. Don't reach for the top of a thing
until the bottom can hold it.

When I make decisions, I look for the order first. What has to be
true before the next thing can be true? Build that. Then build what
comes next.

## Win Conditions

I do not build toward zero. I build toward maximum.

In personal training, rehabilitation focuses on getting a patient
back to activities of daily living — because that is where insurance
coverage ends. The system is built to that ceiling, and the ceiling
is low. My philosophy is different: focus on peak performance even
when the goal is rehabilitation. Zero is a milestone you pass
through on the way to something else. Build toward the best possible
outcome. The rehab happens inside that pursuit automatically.

This principle applies everywhere. A prerequisite is a gate — pass
it and move on. A win condition is a direction — everything pointing
toward the best possible outcome, with intermediate markers that
confirm you're moving rather than finish lines that let you stop.

Every condition required to meet the end goals I have in my life is
a win condition. I am not trying to pass. I am trying to excel.

I also know myself: I need early wins to sustain momentum. Systems
that provide no reward until the end lose me before I get there.
So I sequence win conditions psychologically, not just logically.
Early wins are structural, not optional. This is not weakness — it
is design.

## Fame, Celebrity, and Wealth

Fame is being known for what you have done. Celebrity is being known
for who you are. I want fame if it comes — because it would mean I
built something worth knowing about, and I could keep building.
Celebrity I do not want — it has to be performed and maintained and
can collapse at any moment.

The cleaner version: the only thing better than being rich and
famous is being rich. Wealth is freedom. Fame and celebrity both
carry obligations. I want the freedom.

I build. If recognition arrives as a byproduct, I accept it. I am
not performing for it.

## The Honest Self-Portrait

William James Sidis chose the work over the recognition. He kept
building under pseudonyms while remaining largely anonymous. Albert
Einstein pursued the same kind of work and became Einstein. The
difference was not talent. It was the path fame took.

I want the Einstein outcome. I understand I may be living the Sidis
life for a long time first — or indefinitely. I am naming that
clearly so it does not surprise me.

## My Heroes (By My Definition)

Kendrick Lamar. Charles Bukowski. Maya Angelou. Bob Marley.
Leonardo da Vinci. Albert Einstein. Nikola Tesla. Rick Rubin.
Steve Jobs. William James. Robert Pirsig. Jimmy Carr.

The world may label some of them otherwise. To me, they are all
artists — because they each reached a level where they could create
freely, without being bent by trends or expectations. They earned
their freedom through quality. Then they used it to build toward
something beyond themselves.

That is the model. Earn the freedom. Use it well.

## What I Reject

I do not apologize for anything.

I do not follow trends. I do not create for credit. I do not build
top-heavy. I do not optimize for the appearance of quality over its
substance. I do not aim at celebrity.

I do not confuse comfort with correctness. Staying somewhere because
it is easy and mistake-free is not the same as staying because it
is right.

I do not believe that something must be clean to be useful, or that
something must be toxic to be the best tool for the job. Reality is
not that tidy. The best pan might leach chemicals. The right answer
might have a cost. I prefer to know the cost and choose anyway.

I do not require things to be one thing. Neither timeless nor
trendy. Both, or neither, or something the categories don't cover.
I distrust definitions that close off more than they open.

I do not build toward zero. Zero is not the goal. Zero is a
milestone.

## On Constraints

There are no real constraints on building — only inputs I have not
metabolized yet. A new environment is not a pause. It is material.

Every place I go, every person I encounter, every system I have to
work within — these are inputs. The Peace Corps is a job. It is also
two years of fresh perspective in the Eastern Caribbean, unfamiliar
problems, and people who see the world differently than anyone I
have worked beside before. If anything, it accelerates the work.

Every constraint is an input. Every input is part of the art.

## The Tension

Society does not yet know what I can do. I believe I can do what I
believe I can do. That gap — between what I know and what the world
has seen — is the tension I live in right now.

It is not comfortable. It is not supposed to be. It is the most
honest fuel there is.
